
My career is centered at the intersection of mathematical physics, information
theory, and large-scale scientific computing. I chose Physics to understand how
collective order emerges from chaotic, noisy interactions, whether in physical
particles, biological networks, or computational algorithms. Throughout my
journey, my aim has been to act as a genuine bridge: translating physical
intuition and mathematical rigor into scalable models that explain
high-dimensional dynamics from first principles.

I began my interdisciplinary academic foundation with Non-Equilibrium
Statistical Physics: transport phenomena via the Totally Asymmetric Simple
Exclusion Process (TASEP), and spin-exchange dynamics (conserved Ising,
Kawasaki, and Schelling models) to formulate a theoretical basis for
understanding complex stochastic dynamical systems such as Opinion Dynamics
with Prof. Soham Biswas. I also explored computational methods for the local
detection of community structures in complex networks with Prof. Satu Elisa
Schaeffer.

During my undergraduate years, eager to test theoretical knowledge against
real-world applications, I joined R&D teams where I had the opportunity to lead
computational and algorithmic optimization initiatives at world-class
enterprises, such as Hewlett-Packard (HP) Labs and Oracle. I gained invaluable
insights into the gap between academic formulations and real-world industrial
deployment.

As rewarding as my industry experience was, optimizing commercial workflows
ultimately reignited my intellectual curiosity and explore the fundamental
principles. I moved to Trieste, Italy to join the prestigious Quantitative Life
Sciences (QLS) program at the International Centre for Theoretical Physics
(ICTP) where, alongside the brightest minds, I explored the deep interplay
between Universal Biology and Active Matter, Probability and Information
Theory, Mathematical Economics and Game Theory, Computational Neuroscience, and
Reinforcement Learning.
Working under the guidance of Prof. Jean Barbier, I tackled high-dimensional
statistical inference and rank-one estimation in the spiked Wigner model. I
developed a prior-independent Onsager correction scheduler for Approximate
Message Passing (AMP) algorithms, characterizing Bayes-optimal signal recovery
thresholds and exposing fundamental algorithmic barriers under structured
noise.

To complement this theoretical core, I chose an unconventional, demanding,
concurrent path by earning an honors Master in High Performance Computing
(MHPC) at SISSA, while simultaneously pursuing my PhD in Applied Data Science
and Artificial Intelligence (ADSAI) at the University of Trieste and OGS. This
duality allowed me to architect and deploy, on Italy's flagship Tier-0 CINECA
Leonardo supercomputer, distributed and scalable multi-node GPU Deep Learning
pipelines (using MPI, CUDA, SLURM) for high-throughput scientific data
analysis.
